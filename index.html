    How to cook

    Experiment, watch the food, and use the ticket as your brief.

    Modes and saves

    Service is six timed tickets. Free practice has no orders or scores; form up to four patties and clear the bench. Leave practice returns to service. Pause and Help stop the simulation. Save uses separate service/practice slots; active sessions autosave about every 15 seconds. Resume saved restores paused, with no offline cooking.

    Forming

    Choose blend, mass, thickness, handling, starting temperature, salt, and dimple. Early mixed salt makes a springier patty; surface salt goes on just before cooking. Thick or frozen patties need more centre time and can overcook outside.

    Tickets and selection

    Tickets ask for one to three burgers. Expand for builds; a check means assigned, not cooked. Chips, clicking food, or [/] selects the target. Serve together; resting food can go cold.

    Build and serve

    Cook patties and extras, rest them, then open Build. Add layers in order; Undo layer removes the last and Unpack returns food to the pass. Fresh toppings and sauces go in the stack; buns stay paired. Only placed food counts; missing, raw, or burnt requested food can send it back. Back on the heat reheats unpacked food. Discard topping removes the selection; a bun removes both halves. Finish the top bun, then Cut & serve.

    Camera and view

    Left-drag empty space to orbit; drag food to move it. Wheel/right-drag zooms. Right-click focuses; right-click again backs out. Middle-drag or Shift-left-drag pans. Presets: Room, Oven, Cook, Overhead, Side, Close. R resets; arrows orbit; +/− zoom. Cutaway/C shows inside.

    Cooking model

    Each patty and topping tracks heat, water, fat, and browning. Food heats at pan/grate contact; the pan centre is hotter than its edge, and crowding or cold food pulls it down. Wet surfaces boil before browning. Cold oil cools the pan; pan water warms, evaporates, and can spatter in oil. Cheese can melt, then lose water. 1×–8× changes clock speed, not the model.

    Pan, oven, and actions

    Choose stove, pan, and fat in Heat; use the knob and add fat before loading. Drag or Move to centre/edge. Flip turns; Press squeezes; Smash is for thin patties; Scrape gets under crust. Lid traps heat/steam; Baste adds butter; Off the heat rests the selection. Set an oven temperature, use Oven for an unassembled patty, then rest it. Wash pan needs an empty pan and cools it; on charcoal it wire-brushes hot bars. Wipe stove clears spatter; Open window vents. Menu has Inspector, °C / °F, and sound.

    Charcoal kettle

    Choose Charcoal kettle before heating; it replaces the pan with a grate. The knob is the bottom vent. Wait for glowing coals and hot bars. Bank coals makes hot and cool sides. Add coals early: new coals are cold. Stir clears ash; Empty ash works only when cold. Add wood gives hickory, apple, or mesquite smoke. With the lid on, Lid vent controls airflow and smoke exit; open air burns cleaner, closed vents make sooty smoke. Fat and juice can flare.

    Toppings

    Extras cook on their own footprint. Buns go cut-side down; toast slows juice uptake. Bacon needs rendering before it crisps. A lid steams an egg's top; flipping cooks its second face. On bare bars, a raw egg falls through and burns on the coals; an already-set fried egg can stay, but either face can burn. Onions need moderate heat and stirring; a wet pile softens before browning, and a grate loses slices. Cheese can melt, then lose water, burn, or drop through the grate, so add it late over charcoal. The tray holds four of each extra, two bun pairs, and four cheese slices per patty.

    Stack moisture and pressure

    Hot filling moves juice through the stack and into the bun. Toast slows bun absorption; mayo and lettuce slow flow. Lettuce can wilt and be mentioned in the result, but has no score penalty. Weight above squashes loose lettuce, onions, and bacon most.

    Senses and instruments

    In Utensils, Insert probe reads selected depth; the IR gun shows pan centre; Inspector shows detail. Press test (T) costs juice; Peek (K) costs juice and structure; Hand over it (H) is a relative cue, with different pan/grill scales. Hard mode removes probe, Inspector, and numeric temperatures.

    Sound and cues

    Rough crackle means water is boiling; a quiet hiss means the face is dry enough to brown; lifted food goes quiet. Charcoal adds roar, flare-ups, and smoke; the lid muffles it. Toggle Sound in Menu.

    Service and score

    The clock starts at Yes chef and runs kitchen time; faster playback advances it too. Serve ticket burgers together—resting food cools. Results judge centre doneness after rest, crust, juice, heat, structure, and build. Service records points and tips; practice does not.

    Doneness

    The target is the highest centre temperature reached, including carry-over during rest. Probe depth matters; the outside can disagree. Thickness, starting temperature, heat, flips, presses, and rest change the result. Use the ticket range and watch the centre.

    Useful habits

    Preheat. Shape lightly and use a dimple to limit doming. Thick patties want gentler heat and more flips. Wait for the wet crackle to fade; press intentionally; flip with a crust or juice cue. Keep the pan uncrowded. Rest before building or serving.
