    How this works

    Viewport — left-drag orbits, wheel or right-drag zooms, right-click zooms onto whatever you clicked (right-click again to back out), middle/shift-drag pans. Keys 1–4 are view presets, R resets, arrows orbit. Cutaway (C) slices the patty so you can watch the grey band creep inward.

    Physics — the patty is a stack of ~0.6 mm layers. Each one has its own temperature, water, fat and denatured-protein state. Heat comes in through a contact conductance from the pan (which has its own thermal mass and burner), water boils off at 100 °C and pins the surface there until it dries, juice squeezed out by contracting proteins migrates to the faces, fat melts and drains, and once the surface is dry and hot the Maillard reaction browns it — then pyrolysis blackens it. Colour, shine, steam, smoke, spatter and sizzle are all driven by those numbers.

    Doneness is the peak centre temperature, including carry-over while resting. Rare 49–52, medium-rare 54–57, medium 60–63, medium-well 65–68, well done 71+ °C.

    Tips from real life — preheat properly; don't press a thick patty; a thumb dimple stops it doming; flipping often cooks faster and more evenly; the juice pooling on top is a flip cue; meat releases from the pan on its own once the crust sets; rest before cutting.
